Elasticity of Demand

Responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in price.

Price elasticity of demand — the percentage change in quantity demanded divided by the percentage change in price. Reported as an absolute value.
Ed = (%ΔQd) / (%ΔP)

Reading the value

  • Ed > 1 — elastic. Quantity moves proportionally more than price; revenue falls when price rises.
  • Ed < 1 — inelastic. Buyers absorb the price change. Insulin, petrol, textbooks.
  • Ed = 1 — unit elastic. Revenue is at its maximum here.

What pushes elasticity up

  • Close substitutes exist — one coffee shop of four on the same street.
  • The good is a large share of income.
  • Longer time horizon: substitution takes time, so long-run demand is more elastic than short-run.

Worked example

Price rises 10%, quantity demanded falls 4%. Ed = 4/10 = 0.4, so demand is inelastic and total revenue rises. Check the sign convention before the exam — the marking scheme wants the absolute value.

Exam note

Do not confuse a movement along the curve with a shift of the curve. Elasticity describes the slope behaviour, not a change in the determinants of demand.
